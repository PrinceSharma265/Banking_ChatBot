Personal Loan Policy

1. Eligibility criteria
The bank extends personal loans to individuals aged between 21 years and 60 years. The applicant must be a salaried employee drawing a minimum gross salary of Rs. 25,000 per month. Self-employed professionals and business owners are evaluated on a case-by-case basis, subject to satisfactory vintage of business and tax compliance. Employment history should show at least two years with the current employer or in the current line of business.

2. Loan amount range
Personal loans are sanctioned from a minimum of Rs. 50,000 up to a maximum of Rs. 40,00,000. The final amount depends on the applicant's income, existing liabilities, credit score, and repayment capacity. The bank may offer lower limits to applicants with shorter credit histories or higher existing debt obligations.

3. Interest rates
Interest rates for personal loans range from 10.5% to 18% per annum. The exact rate is determined after assessment of the applicant’s credit bureau score, employment stability, existing debt service ratio, and overall risk profile. Salaried applicants with strong credit history may qualify for rates at the lower end, while those with weaker credit profiles may be offered rates towards the higher end.

4. Tenure options
Tenure options for personal loans range from 12 months to 60 months. The bank encourages borrowers to select a tenure that balances affordability and total interest cost. Shorter tenures reduce the total interest outgo, while longer tenures lower the EMI burden.

5. Processing fee
A processing fee of 1% to 2% of the sanctioned loan amount is levied. The exact fee is disclosed at the time of sanction and is usually dependent on the loan amount and customer segment. Processing fees are payable upfront or may be recovered from the loan proceeds, as per the bank’s policy.

6. Documents required
The following documents are required for personal loan approval:
- Aadhaar card for identity proof and address verification
- PAN card for income tax compliance and KYC purposes
- Latest three months salary slips for salaried individuals
- Last six months bank statements for salary account and other financial transactions
- Form 16 or income tax returns where applicable
- Passport size photographs
Additional documents may be requested based on the applicant’s employment type or source of income.

7. Prepayment charges
Prepayment charges for personal loans are structured to protect the bank’s interest margin. If the borrower makes a prepayment within the first 12 months, a charge of 2% on the amount prepaid is applicable after six months from loan disbursement. No prepayment charges are levied after 12 months, allowing greater flexibility for borrowers to close the loan early without additional cost.

8. EMI calculation example
For illustrative purposes, consider a personal loan of Rs. 10,00,000 at an interest rate of 12% per annum for a tenure of 48 months. The monthly EMI is calculated using the standard amortization formula, resulting in an EMI of approximately Rs. 26,46,000 divided by 48 equals around Rs. 26,350. Total interest payable over the tenure would be approximately Rs. 2,63,000. This example is indicative and the actual EMI will vary depending on the sanctioned rate and tenure.

Home Loan Policy

1. Eligibility
The bank offers home loans to individuals aged between 21 years and 65 years at the time of loan sanction. Both salaried and self-employed applicants are eligible. Salaried applicants must demonstrate stable employment and consistent salary credit. Self-employed professionals and business owners must provide evidence of business continuity, positive cash flows, and tax returns for the last two years.

2. Loan amount
Home loan amounts range from a minimum of Rs. 5,00,000 to a maximum of Rs. 5,00,00,000. The sanctioned amount depends on the property's valuation, applicant’s income, credit score, and repayment capacity. Loans for affordable housing and first-time buyers may be sanctioned under special schemes.

3. Interest rates
Interest rates for home loans vary from 8.5% to 12% per annum. The rate offered is based on the borrower’s credit profile, loan amount, tenure, and prevailing market conditions. The Bank offers both fixed and floating rate options, with floating rate linked to its benchmark lending rate.

4. Tenure
Home loan tenure ranges from 5 years to 30 years. Applicants are encouraged to choose a tenure that fits their long-term financial plan. Longer tenure reduces monthly installments but increases total interest cost, while shorter tenure increases EMI but lowers overall interest paid.

5. Processing fee
A processing fee of 0.5% of the loan amount is applicable on home loans. The fee is payable at the time of loan disbursement. In some cases, a minimum fee amount may be stipulated along with the percentage.

6. LTV ratio
The loan-to-value (LTV) ratio for home loans is up to 80% of the property value. This means the borrower must contribute at least 20% of the property value as a down payment. For certain categories such as affordable housing or government-sponsored schemes, the bank may consider higher LTV ratios subject to regulatory guidelines.

7. Documents required
Home loan documentation includes:
- Aadhaar card and PAN card
- Proof of income such as salary slips, bank statements, Form 16 for salaried applicants
- Income tax returns and profit-and-loss statements for self-employed applicants
- Property documents including sale agreement, title deed, and approved building plan
- Property valuation report and NOC from builder or society where applicable
- Passport size photographs and identity proof of co-applicants
Additional legal and technical documentation may be required during the final sanction stage.

Education Loan Policy

1. Eligible courses and institutions
Education loans are available for professional and higher education courses undertaken at recognized institutions in India and abroad. Eligible courses include engineering, medical, management, law, architecture, pharmacy, and other recognized degree and diploma programs. The bank also supports select vocational and skill development courses offered by approved institutions.

2. Loan amount
The bank extends education loans up to Rs. 50,00,000 for studies abroad. For domestic education, the loan amount is determined based on the actual cost of education, tuition fees, living expenses, and other admissible expenses. Abroad education loans cover tuition fees, examination fees, travel costs, and maintenance allowance.

3. Interest rates
Interest rates for education loans range from 9% to 14% per annum. The final rate depends on the borrower’s credit profile, margin money requirement, course type, and institution. Government-backed or priority sector education loans may carry concessional rates.

4. Moratorium period
The bank offers a moratorium period covering the course duration plus an additional grace period of six months after course completion. During the moratorium period, repayment of the principal may be deferred, but interest may accrue and be payable later as per the loan agreement.

5. Repayment after course completion
Repayment of education loans begins after the completion of the course and the end of the moratorium period. The borrower may choose a repayment tenure that generally extends up to 10 years from the end of the moratorium. Loan repayment is structured through monthly EMIs, and prepayment of the education loan is permitted without penalty after the stipulated moratorium.

Vehicle Loan Policy

1. New and used vehicle loans
The bank provides loans for both new and used vehicles. New vehicle loans are available for cars, two-wheelers, and commercial vehicles purchased from authorized dealerships. Used vehicle loans are sanctioned against pre-owned vehicles subject to age and condition criteria.

2. Interest rates
Interest rates for vehicle loans range from 7.5% to 12% per annum. The exact rate is set based on the borrower’s credit profile, vehicle age, down payment, and loan tenure. New vehicle loans typically receive more competitive rates than used vehicle loans.

3. Tenure
Vehicle loan tenure ranges from 12 months to 84 months. For two-wheelers, the maximum tenure is generally up to 60 months, while for passenger cars and commercial vehicles the tenure can extend up to 84 months depending on the loan amount and applicant profile.

4. Down payment requirements
A down payment is required for vehicle loans, typically ranging from 10% to 25% of the vehicle purchase price. For new vehicles, the borrower is expected to contribute at least 15% of the value, while for used vehicles the down payment may be higher based on vehicle age and resale value. The bank calculates the loan amount after deducting the down payment and any applicable subsidies.

The bank's lending policies are governed by prudential norms, regulatory guidelines, and internal credit assessment procedures. All loan approvals are subject to verification of documents, credit appraisal, and satisfaction of the bank’s risk management standards.
